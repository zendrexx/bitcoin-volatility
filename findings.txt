Log-likelihood and AIC pick the SAME winner in every cell. The parameter
penalty is a rounding error next to the LL gap: Model A -> Model B adds 6
free parameters under diag (20 -> 26) and 12 under full (23 -> 35), so AIC
moves by 12 or 24 while the LL term moves by thousands. 99.9% of every AIC
difference is the LL term. AIC here is essentially -2 x LL, NOT a
meaningful complexity penalty - do not present LL, AIC and BIC as three
independent confirmations.

SECTION 12.  THE 10 OCTOBER 2025 CRASH
==============================================================================

EXCLUDED_WINDOWS is empty, so this event IS in the sample.

At 21:15 UTC on 10 Oct 2025, one 15-minute bar:
  COIN       RETURN   HIGH-LOW RANGE
  ----------------------------------
  LTC       -30.14%           74.72%
  DOGE      -17.95%           63.96%
  ETH        -3.87%            9.87%
  BTC        -3.42%            9.69%

WHY IT BREAKS THE MODEL

Standard-scaled, the LTC bar sits 53 sigma from the mean. A Gaussian's
density at 53 sigma is e^-1406, which is exactly zero in floating point.
The model cannot place that bar in an ordinary state - doing so sends the
likelihood of the whole dataset to -infinity. Its only escape is to build
a state centred on the bar with near-zero variance. And because Gaussian
likelihood grows WITHOUT BOUND as variance shrinks onto a single point,
EM is actively rewarded for doing this.

So a 3-state model does not give you 3 regimes plus a crash. It gives:

  LTC 15m Q4, Model C, decoded states:
    state      bars   % of data                  what it is
    -------------------------------------------------------
    0         8,812      99.89%          the entire quarter
    1             9       0.10%               the aftermath
    2             1       0.01%               the crash bar

  Two of the three hidden states are spent on ten bars. There is no
  Bull/Sideways/Bear left to report for that cell. The crash is not
  MODELLED, it is MEMORISED.

WHAT IT COSTS

  48 of 384 fits are degenerate or failed (6 failed outright).
  Every single one is a Q4 DOGE or LTC cell:

    QUARTER        CLEAN  BROKEN
    ----------------------------
    Q1                96       0
    Q2                96       0
    Q3                96       0
    Q4                48      48

    Q4 by coin     CLEAN  BROKEN
    ----------------------------
    BTC               24       0
    ETH               24       0
    DOGE               0      24
    LTC                0      24

  BTC and ETH absorb the crash without collapsing. DOGE and LTC do not.

==============================================================================
SECTION 13.  SUMMARY OF FINDINGS
==============================================================================

 1. Q1 2025 IS THE RESULT. With covariance_type='full', the volume model
    beats Model A in 9 of 12 coin/timeframe cells - and every one of those
    9 is a clean fit. On BTC+ETH it is 6 of 6, average +7.90% on AIC.
    Best cells: ETH 5m +12.76%, ETH 15m +12.11%, ETH 30m +9.18%.

 2. COVARIANCE TYPE DECIDES THE ANSWER. Under 'full' the volume model wins
    20 of 48 cells. Under 'diag' it wins essentially none. Same data, same
    features, same seed, same iteration cap. This is the single most
    consequential setting in the codebase and it must be justified in
    Chapter 3, not left as an implementation detail.

 3. Why: volume and bar range are correlated 0.25-0.76 WITHIN each hidden
    state. Diag covariance assumes that correlation is zero, so it throws
    away the channel the volume signal actually travels through. Full
    covariance is the general case; diag is a restriction of it.

 4. Q3 is a clean negative: 0 of 12 cells, all clean fits, average -11.72%.
    Volume never helps in Q3 on any coin or timeframe.

 5. Q4 IS NOT A RESULT, and the current run proves it from the inside.
    Counting every cell, Model B wins 7 of 12. Counting only cells where
    no hidden state collapsed, it wins 2 of 6 - BTC 15m +0.68% and ETH 30m
    +0.63%, both under one percent. All five of the extra 'wins' are
    exactly the five degenerate DOGE/LTC cells. Not most of them. All.
    Final ordering: Q1 >> Q2 > Q4 ~ Q3.

 6. The crash costs 48 of 384 fits. With EXCLUDED_WINDOWS empty, 48 fits
    are degenerate or failed (6 failed outright). All 48 are Q4 DOGE/LTC.
    Q1, Q2 and Q3 are 100% clean; BTC and ETH are 100% clean in every
    quarter. The damage is perfectly localised.

 7. Log-likelihood, AIC and BIC agree in every cell. They are not three
    independent checks - the parameter penalty is 12-24 while the LL term
    moves by thousands, so AIC is essentially -2 x LL. Do not present them
    as mutual confirmation.

 8. Comparing LL or AIC across models with different feature counts is not
    valid. Each extra standardised feature costs about 1.42 nats per bar
    before it earns anything back. Models A-D are densities over 2, 3, 3
    and 4 dimensions, so their likelihoods are not on the same scale.

 9. Most of volume's likelihood contribution is REAL regime information,
    not the heavy-tail artifact. Refitting Model B with the volume column
    permuted three ways splits the contribution into:
      29.3% marginal shape (the heavy tail - the artifact)
       7.7% short-run persistence
      63.0% alignment with the regime sequence
    This split is stable at 63% alignment across every configuration
    tested - diag or full, crash in or out.

10. Volume tracks regimes even where it loses on AIC. With regimes fitted
    WITHOUT volume, the high-volatility regime shows strongly positive
    volume surprise in 12 of 12 BTC cells, and Low and Medium are both
    negative in 12 of 12. This holds in Q3 and Q4 too. No likelihood is
    involved, so it is immune to the objection in finding 8.

11. Adding volume changes 29-51% of decoded bar labels (A-vs-B agreement
    49.3-71.4%). Volume is not a cosmetic addition to the segmentation
    once the model can actually use it.

12. Models C and D always score worst on LL, AIC and BIC. Rolling
    volatility is a smoothed, highly autocorrelated feature - it costs a
    dimension without adding independent information.

13. LTC has up to 2.9% of 5m bars with exactly zero return (BTC: 0.1-0.5%),
    from tick-size granularity. It is the only coin where the volume model
    never wins in any quarter, and one of the two whose Q4 fits break.

14. All 384 fits converged well inside the iteration cap - maximum 239
    iterations used of 500 permitted, median 50. n_iter is not a binding
    constraint and is not worth tuning.

==============================================================================
SECTION 14.  WHAT TO DO

1. Justify covariance_type='full' in Chapter 3. The argument is: diag
   forces conditional independence given the state and discards the
   volume/range correlation the thesis question is about; full is the
   general case and diag is a restriction of it. Both pipelines are
   currently set to 'full' and must stay in sync.

2. Report the covariance sensitivity as a finding, not a footnote. Table 1
   vs Table 2 shows that a standard, rarely-discussed implementation
   choice reverses the central result. That is a real contribution.

3. REWRITE THE Q4 CLAIM. Any draft text saying volume helps in Q4 is
   wrong. Use the wording drafted at the end of Section 12, which reports
   Q4 both ways and declines to claim it under either.

4. Add a degeneracy guard: reject or flag any fit where a state holds
   under ~1% of bars. Right now four Q4 cells produce publishable-looking
   LL/AIC/BIC numbers from collapsed models, and main.py exports them to
   data/processed/ without complaint.

5. Decide and document the crash policy. EXCLUDED_WINDOWS is currently
   empty, so the event is in the sample. That is defensible provided the
   degenerate Q4 cells are excluded from the analysis and reported as
   such. The alternative - winsorising returns at a stated threshold -
   keeps the event in the timeline without the state collapse.

6. Do not present LL, AIC and BIC as three separate confirmations. They
   agree by construction here. Say so, and pick one.

7. Stop using cross-model LL/AIC to argue a feature belongs. Use Table 8
   (volume surprise) and the permutation decomposition instead - both are
   independent of the likelihood framework.

8. State the LTC/DOGE tick-resolution limitation in Chapter 3, and treat
   LTC as the weakest leg of the cross-asset robustness claim.

9. Add error handling to main.py before pointing it at anything but BTC.
